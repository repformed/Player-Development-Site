What online learning can do

Use screen time to prepare a better question.

A video or diagram is most useful when you know what you are trying to learn. Begin with one idea—what a first down means, how a diagram labels movement, or what information a lineman needs before a snap. After reading, close the page and explain the idea without looking. If you cannot explain it yet, return to that part rather than racing to another lesson.

Reading supports understanding; it cannot observe your physical technique or replace your team’s coaching, supervision and playbook. These pages are for learning away from contact practice. They do not prescribe a workout or a universal blocking assignment.

For athletes and parents

Choose by understanding, not just age.

Read the parent guide to choosing online football support.

A new adult player may need the same vocabulary as a young beginner. An experienced player may know the terms but still need help explaining a particular responsibility. Start with what the learner can explain today.

  • Parents: ask “What did that symbol mean?” rather than asking for a perfect score. Do not submit child names, videos or personal details through the interest form.
  • Beginners: use the answer explanation even after a correct choice. The reason matters more than guessing.
  • Returning players: connect the lesson to your own team’s terminology and ask your coach where it differs.

The proposed membership path

Four levels. One learning foundation.

Foundation

Two age-appropriate football-IQ videos and two lesson quizzes per week. No drills.

Fundamentals

Everything proposed in Foundation, plus two movement sessions per week.

Position Development

Everything proposed in Fundamentals, plus two additional position drills each week for one selected position.

Elite Coaching

Everything proposed in Position Development, plus two corrections per athlete per month. Each submitted clip is up to three minutes; the turnaround target is three business days, not a guarantee.
